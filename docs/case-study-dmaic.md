# DMAIC case study: drawing-revision review

> **Data disclaimer.** The only real figure in this document is the headline:
> the drawing-revision review at D.L. Engineering took about 2 hours before
> automation and about 30 minutes after it. **Every other number, the specification
> limit, the defect categories and the "template change" are illustrative.** They
> are generated by [`dmaic_case_study.py`](dmaic_case_study.py) from a seeded
> random number generator so the SPC module can be demonstrated end to end. They
> are not D.L. Engineering measurements, and the automation is not described here
> beyond "automated checks of the revision package". Re-run the script on real
> timestamps before quoting any capability figure.

Reproduce everything below with `python docs/dmaic_case_study.py`. The tests in
`tests/test_spc.py` fail if this document's quoted numbers drift from the code.

## Define

- **Process:** reviewing a drawing-revision package (revised drawing, revision
  history, title block, BOM) before release.
- **Problem:** review took about 2 hours per package, delaying release.
- **CTQ (critical to quality):** review cycle time in minutes.
- **Requirement (assumed):** a review finishes within **USL = 60 minutes**. There
  is no lower limit, so capability is one-sided.
- **Goal:** about 30 minutes, with a stable process (no special-cause signals).

## Measure

Illustrative data: 20 weeks x 5 reviews per week, before and after automation
(100 reviews each). Weekly groups of 5 are the rational subgroups for an X-bar-R
chart (`spc.xbar_r_chart`).

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Mean review time | 118.9 min | 30.0 min |
| Standard deviation | 32.8 min | 9.0 min |
| Average weekly range (R-bar) | 82.8 min | 20.6 min |
| X-bar limits (LCL / UCL) | 71.1 / 166.7 | 18.1 / 41.9 |
| R-chart UCL | 175.1 | 43.6 |
| Western Electric signals | none | none |

Both processes are **in statistical control** on the X-bar-R chart (no weekly
mean or range outside its limits, and no Western Electric pattern). Capability is
therefore meaningful: the "before" process is stably slow, not erratic.

## Analyze

**Where does the time go? Pareto of review findings** (illustrative counts per 100
packages, `spc.pareto_table` / `spc.pareto_chart`):

| Finding | Count | % | Cumulative % | Vital few |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revision letter mismatch | 38 | 38 | 38 | yes |
| Title block / BOM mismatch | 27 | 27 | 65 | yes |
| Missing revision cloud | 14 | 14 | 79 | yes |
| Tolerance not updated | 9 | 9 | 88 | yes |
| Stale referenced drawing | 7 | 7 | 95 | |
| Other | 5 | 5 | 100 | |

The first three categories are consistency checks between documents, the kind of
comparison a script can do exactly and a person does slowly. They account for 79%
of findings and are the automation targets (the fourth category crosses the 80%
line and is also marked "vital few").

**Investigate the causes.** Copy the [fishbone and 5-whys worksheet](root-cause-worksheet.md)
to investigate a selected category. Its six fishbone branches organize possible
causes; the why chain, evidence log and countermeasure plan support testing them.
The Pareto ranking identifies frequent findings, not their causes or share of
review time. The worksheet's starting example is an unverified hypothesis, not a
completed investigation at D.L. Engineering. The separate
[completed synthetic investigation](root-cause-investigation.md) replays the
leading category, tests a cache-selection cause, rejects alternatives and checks
a countermeasure against the same inputs and a follow-up batch. Its modeled
times are separate from this study's illustrative review-time samples.

**How capable was the process?** Review times are right-skewed (they cannot go
below zero and have a long tail), so a normal model is a poor fit. DistroFit's
`fit_candidates` ranks Normal, Gamma, Log-Normal and Weibull (Min) on held-out
likelihood; Weibull wins on both samples. The family scores are close (held-out
NLL 4.986 vs 5.048 for Normal before), so treat the winner as "adequate", not
proven. `spc.capability_from_fit` then applies the ISO 22514-2 percentile method
using that winner.

| Before | Normal theory | Best fit (Weibull), percentile method |
|---|---|---|
| Ppk (USL = 60) | -0.60 | -0.58 |
| Model share above USL | | 99.2% (observed 98 of 100) |

Ppk below zero means the *median* review already exceeds the limit. The process
is not merely incapable, it is centred outside the specification.

## Improve

Automated checks of the revision package replace the manual document comparison
(this is the real intervention; its internals are not modelled here). In the
illustrative data this appears as a shift of the mean from 118.9 to 30.0 minutes
(-75%) and a narrower spread (standard deviation 32.8 to 9.0).

## Control

**Before / after capability (USL = 60 minutes, illustrative data):**

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Mean (min) | 118.9 | 30.0 |
| Ppk, normal theory | -0.60 | 1.11 |
| Cpk, normal theory (R-bar/d2 within-sigma) | -0.55 | 1.13 |
| Ppk, best fit (Weibull), percentile method | -0.58 | **0.97** |
| 99.865th percentile of review time | 225.3 min | 61.0 min |
| Model share above USL | 99.2% | 0.18% (1,820 ppm) |
| Observed reviews above USL | 98 of 100 | 0 of 100 |

Two honest readings of this table:

1. The improvement is large and unambiguous: Ppk goes from about -0.6 to about 1.
2. The "after" process is **improved but not yet capable by the usual 1.33
   criterion**. Normal theory (1.11) overstates it relative to the skew-aware
   percentile estimate (0.97), because the fitted right tail reaches 61 minutes
   at the 99.865th percentile, just past the limit. Zero of 100 observed reviews
   exceeded 60 minutes, but 100 observations cannot resolve a 0.18% tail; this is
   exactly the case where the fitted distribution is more informative than a
   count.

**Ongoing monitoring: I-MR chart.** After handover, limits are fixed from the first
30 stable post-improvement reviews (`spc.imr_chart(values, baseline=30)`):
centre 30.4 min, limits 10.7 to 50.1 min, moving-range UCL 24.2 min. Reviews 31 to
40 then include an **illustrative** 15-minute increase, as if a template change had
slowed the check. The chart responds as designed:

- Western Electric rule 4 (8 in a row above the centre line) fires at review 37.
- Rule 3 (4 of 5 beyond 1 sigma) fires from review 34, rule 2 (2 of 3 beyond
  2 sigma) from review 36 and rule 1 (beyond 3 sigma) at reviews 31 and 39.
- The first 30 reviews produce no signals.

Rules 2 to 4 detect a sustained shift of this size within a few points, well before
a person would notice a slow drift in a stopwatch average. The control plan is:
keep charting each review, investigate any Western Electric signal, and recompute
baseline limits only after a deliberate, verified process change.

### Caveats

- The seed (2029) was chosen from a scan of ten because its baseline phases have no
  false alarms. Other seeds can signal by chance (about 1 in 370 points on a
  normal in-control I chart for rule 1 alone, more once all four rules apply, and
  skewed data signals high more often than low).
- The individuals chart assumes roughly symmetric data; with strongly skewed times
  consider charting a transformed value or using percentile-based limits.
- Pp, Ppk and Cpk here use a sample of 100 per phase. Capability indices carry
  sampling error that is not shown; do not read the second decimal as meaningful.
- The assumed USL of 60 minutes is not a D.L. Engineering requirement.
